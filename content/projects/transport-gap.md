*Six preprints, September 2026. All six were produced with substantial AI assistance under my direction — the full statement is at the [bottom of this page](#status-and-attribution).*

## The program

Every known route to global regularity for the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations stops at the same place: the only unconditional bound is the energy inequality, which is supercritical by half a derivative, and no scale-invariant quantity is known to be bounded a priori. This program does not close that gap. It asks what a quantity that did close it would have to look like — first by listing conditions such a quantity must satisfy, then by following the equations term by term and object by object to see where an estimate could live, and finally by merging what that search turned up into a single specification.

**Where it ends.** In a reformulation, not a solution: what must be controlled is not the circulation, which is not bounded, but its continuity in time at the critical level. The last paper says in so many words that it reformulates the problem and does not solve it.

## The six papers

**Eight Conditions on the Missing Estimate for the Three-Dimensional Navier–Stokes Equations** — 9 pp, note. Assembles, from results that are mostly not its own, eight conditions that any quantity deciding the problem must satisfy, each tied to a theorem or a construction that rules out the alternative. Two carry new content. A parity condition: Navier–Stokes preserves mirror symmetry and helicity is odd under reflection, so helicity and every pseudoscalar invariant vanish identically on an invariant class in which the known blowup constructions for model equations are carried out — Miller's Fourier-restricted model, which keeps the energy equality and the exact enstrophy-growth identity and blows up, has helicity identically zero. A transport condition, read off Miller's one-parameter family of strain models, all sharing the enstrophy identity and constraint space of the true equation: the family is regular at one end and blows up in its interior, which places the burden of any regularity proof on advection. Along the way it records an elementary criterion: a smooth solution stays regular as long as a single helical component lies in the scale-critical class L∞ₜḢ^{1/2} ∩ L²ₜḢ^{3/2}. [Preprint](/preprints/eight-conditions.pdf)

**Transport and the Supercriticality Gap in 3D Navier–Stokes, I** — 9 pp. Examines the strain equation DₜS = −S² − Ω² − ∇²p + νΔS term by term and asks which term could carry an estimate closing the gap between the energy level and the critical level. Two elementary lemmas show that where strain produces enstrophy and dominates rotation, every local term, the centrifugal one included, drives the eigenvalues toward the restricted-Euler singular configuration; the anisotropic part of the pressure Hessian is the only inviscid term that can oppose either. At the level of norms the pressure behaves differently: its contribution to the growth of the strain norms changes sign exactly at L², through a single unit-free number Λ, and direct simulations at 128³ find Λ < 0 in every dynamical state, so the pressure slows the growth of the norms above L² and accelerates it below, including the critical L^{3/2}. Advection contributes nothing to the budget of any Lebesgue norm, so if the equations are regular, the regularizing mechanism Miller's model family requires must act through transport, which no single-time norm budget can see. Lagrangian tracers show each strain-producing event yields a bounded number of compression e-folds and ends without viscosity, by roll-up or by pressure-driven flattening; the missing estimate must control the escalation from one event to the next, not the amplification within one. [Preprint](/preprints/transport-gap-1.pdf)

**Transport and the Supercriticality Gap in 3D Navier–Stokes, II: The Flow Map** — 8 pp. Moves to the only object that records transport, the flow map, a curve in the group SDiff of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms, and makes the target precise in three statements. By the stochastic Lagrangian representation of Constantin and Iyer and the Beale–Kato–Majda criterion, a smooth solution blows up exactly when the gradient of its stochastic flow map does — a scale-invariant quantity. The geometry the energy controls cannot see deformation: by Shnirelman the L² diameter of the volume-preserving diffeomorphism group of the cube is finite, while for s > 5/2 the right-invariant H^s distance controls the Lipschitz constant and the diameter is infinite. And an a priori Lagrangian bound does exist — the estimate of Crippa and De Lellis, transferred to the stochastic flow — but it is exactly as supercritical as the energy, so Lagrangian bookkeeping by itself gains nothing. What remains is a precise question: an estimate on the Lusin–Lipschitz profile of the stochastic flow that uses the Cauchy–Weber coupling of transport and Biot–Savart. [Preprint](/preprints/transport-gap-2-flow-map.pdf)

**Transport and the Supercriticality Gap in 3D Navier–Stokes, III: The Ledger** — 8 pp. Writes that coupling as a ledger. Combining the stochastic Cauchy formula of Constantin and Iyer with the Biot–Savart law, the strain felt by a fluid particle is a singular integral, over an independent copy of the stochastic flow, of the stretched initial vorticity of all other labels; in these variables the viscous equations are a mean-field system. An exact Fourier characterization of the strain supplied by sources in a distance shell makes the ledger computable, and the known blowup and growth constructions for neighbouring equations are listed by the property of the ledger each one alters. Two registered numerical campaigns follow: events ending in roll-up forward production to the next smaller scale while events ending in pressure-driven flattening mostly do not, no production escalates at the critical rate, and sources at one half to two Taylor lengths pay most of the compression. The escalation between events is written as an exact bookkeeping identity for the event Reynolds number, which states the missing estimate as a question about chains of events. [Preprint](/preprints/transport-gap-3-ledger.pdf)

**Transport and the Supercriticality Gap in 3D Navier–Stokes, IV: Events** — 17 pp. Defines Part III's events rigorously and follows its question — whether the escalation of intensity per halving of scale stays below the critical value 2 — as far as it goes. In Lagrangian variables an event is a unit of pair stretching of the stochastic flow, each costing a unit of ∫‖∇u‖\_{L^∞} dt, so a singularity is infinitely many units in finite time. In frequency space a band at the viscous edge must, given only the energy, concentrate on a set of effective dimension at most one, while concentration above dimension one gives regularity through the criterion of Yoneda, Goto and Tsuruhashi. The currency of the ledger is circulation: by the stochastic Kelvin theorem it is never created, only compressed from larger scales and lost to the noise average, and every known blowup construction for a neighbouring equation lacks either a circulation theorem or the average. In the exact reduction of a vortex tube in an external linear strain it proves a sharp viscous threshold — the mean-square radius cannot fall below 2ν/γ, a structure cannot compress itself, and only axial stretching compresses — and registered simulations find identified tubes at this radius, compressed mainly by the collective background. It ends with the anatomy of a collapse at any rate: a Navier–Stokes realization needs suppliers of total circulation Reynolds number at least 8π within a few core radii, and a Type II collapse must either shrink below the parabolic scale, where it rescales to a bounded eternal solution, or gather circulation. [Preprint](/preprints/transport-gap-4-events.pdf)

**What Must Be Conserved: Thirteen Conditions on the Missing Quantity for the Three-Dimensional Navier–Stokes Equations** — 8 pp, note. Merges the eight conditions of the first note with the five the transport series added — the quantity must credit advection, which every integral of a pointwise function of the velocity gradient ignores; must not rest on invariance under volume-preserving transport alone; must use both the exact transport of circulation and its viscous averaging; and must control both ways circulation can be delivered to a point, by gathering and by the arrival of a self-sustaining cluster of vortices — and tests the known candidates against all thirteen. Three known results then fall into place: a theorem of Enciso, Peralta-Salas and Torres de Lizaur shows nothing conserved by transport alone is informative on mirror-symmetric flows; the heat-smoothed circulation Reynolds number at every scale is equivalent to the norm of the largest scale-critical space, Ḃ^{−1}\_{∞,∞}, where the equations are ill-posed; and by a theorem of Cheskidov and Shvydkoy a solution stays regular unless that norm jumps by a fixed multiple of the viscosity — in circulation language, the arrival at a point of a lump of circulation of order ν. What must be conserved is therefore not the circulation but its continuity in time at the critical level. [Preprint](/preprints/thirteen-conditions.pdf)

## Status and attribution

**Proof status.** The two notes assemble mostly known results and say so; their new content is the parity and transport conditions, and the first note's identities are checked numerically by a script. Part II's statements are proved, largely by combining known theorems. Parts I, III and IV combine elementary lemmas and proved statements with direct simulations, and every numerical hypothesis in them was registered before the run that tested it — Part I reports the failed hypotheses alongside the passed ones, and Part III states the amendments made before its runs. None of the six resolves the regularity problem, and none claims to.

**Attribution.** Each paper's acknowledgement states the division of labour, and those are the precise statements. In outline: the direction of each is mine — including the decisions to state the problem as a list of conditions on a missing quantity, to examine the strain equation term by term, to interrogate the geometry of SDiff through the flow map, to read the transport as an accounting problem, and to formalize events in Lagrangian variables first. The literature search, the derivations, the proofs, the numerical campaigns and a first draft of each paper were produced with substantial AI assistance under my direction, and I am responsible for the contents, including any errors. Not yet independently reviewed; corrections welcome.
