*Five preprints, September 2026. All five were produced with substantial AI assistance under my direction — the full statement is at the [bottom of this page](#status-and-attribution).*

## The question

By Arnold's theorem the Euler equations are not just a bilinear operator obeying an energy identity: they are the Euler–Arnold equation of a Lie algebra with an inertia operator. Tao's averaged Navier–Stokes equation replaces the Euler nonlinearity with an averaged operator that blows up in finite time, and it satisfies the same energy identity. The sequence asks whether that operator can be an Euler–Arnold operator — whether the blowup mechanism is compatible with the geometric structure the Euler equations actually have, or only with the energy identity they happen to satisfy. The answer, across the first four papers, is that it is not — for Tao's operator, and for translation-covariant symbols. It is not a statement that no Euler–Arnold operator blows up: that stronger reading is false, since a second theorem of Tao produces one whose inertia operator is inhomogeneous of the same order as Euler's. Paper I §7 marks out what survives, the isotropic translation-covariant case. The first three press the energy identity as far as it goes and stop short of the general case; the fourth changes the invariant and settles it. The fifth answers a different question about the same family — one Tao raised and left open — and is not a rigidity theorem at all.

## The five papers

**I. Rigidity of the Euler–Arnold Form under Energy-Preserving Deformation** — 19 pp. Two classes of deformation of the Euler vertex are treated: triad reweightings, and vertices twisted by scalar Fourier multipliers on the three legs. On SDiff(T³) the energy-preserving triad reweightings that remain Euler–Arnold are exactly the global rescalings, and the family does not reopen for anisotropic, matrix-valued inertia operators; on Zeitlin's su(N) truncation of SDiff(T²) the corresponding family is two-dimensional instead, which is what gives the three-dimensional statement its content. Tao's operator lies in neither class and is reached separately: because his profiles all live in one thin annulus, a cascade triple is compatible with the Euler bracket only if it carries three distinct species, and twelve of the sixteen non-zero structure constants of his Table 1 carry fewer. Corollary 6.5 sharpens this — the twelve include every coupling that moves energy between scales, so what survives is single-scale and provably bounded. Section 7 states the scope: the known routes to blowup for such a geodesic flow either break the homogeneity of the metric, as Tao's second construction does, or drive the Jacobian of the Lagrangian flow map to zero, which volume preservation forbids — so the translation-covariant case in which these symbol arguments are stated is the one that remains open. [Preprint](/preprints/euler-arnold-rigidity.pdf)

**II. The Isotropic Directional Obstruction for Cascade Operators** — 14 pp. The four couplings that pass Paper I's frequency-support test are settled in the isotropic category, by an argument using the directional structure of the Euler vertex and nothing about frequency geometry: on any non-degenerate triad, no rank-one tensor is an isotropic Euler–Arnold vertex. Since a basic cascade operator contributes exactly one rank-one tensor per block, no basic cascade operator is an isotropic Euler–Arnold operator — irrespective of species count, of the thin annulus, and of whether its frequencies close up. For general anisotropic inertia operators the complementary statement is proved: no block of an Euler–Arnold vertex vanishes faster than κ⁻¹ in the joint spread κ of the three inertia operators, and that rate is sharp, while Tao's surviving coupling needs a block to vanish identically. The paper also fixes the shape any treatment of finite sums must take — the only linear functionals vanishing on the Euler–Arnold class are the componentwise energy identity, which every cascade operator satisfies by construction, so the sum case cannot be decided linearly, nor on one triad at all. [Preprint](/preprints/isotropic-directional-obstruction.pdf)

**III. Cross-Triad Rigidity for Cascade Operators** — 8 pp. What Paper II leaves is whether one fixed coefficient set can reproduce an Euler–Arnold vertex on *all* triads at once. For a two-species cascade this is a gauge-trivialisation problem, answered negatively for every scalar inertia multiplier. A first invariant — the ratios of the blocks' Cayley hyperdeterminants — excludes all exponents but s = 0 and s = −2, Euler and its vorticity dual; that degeneracy is shown to be structural rather than accidental, since on the relevant stratum the hyperdeterminant reduces to the product of the block's four entries. It is also shown to be only a weakness of that invariant: the correct condition, that all the vertices lie in a single GL(2)³ orbit, fails at s = 0 and s = −2 as well. The proof turns on an invariant *condition* rather than an invariant function — the rank of a flattening — which is blind to the degeneracy that silences the hyperdeterminant, and settles all scalar multipliers at once. [Preprint](/preprints/cross-triad-rigidity.pdf)

**IV. Momentum Helicity Separates Cascade Operators from Euler–Arnold Operators** — 9 pp. Papers I–III are all built on the energy identity, which is exactly the invariant Tao's cancellation condition is designed to preserve, so no linear functional can separate the two classes — that is Paper II's Corollary 5.4, and it is why Paper III ends at a dimension count rather than a decision. This paper changes the invariant. Three-dimensional ideal flow conserves a second quantity that Tao's condition does not impose: the helicity of the momentum m = Au, a Casimir of the Lie–Poisson structure and therefore conserved by every Euler–Arnold flow, whatever the inertia operator. The resulting condition collapses to linear algebra — on k⊥ the curl acts as i|k|J for a quarter turn J, and SJS = det(S)J for symmetric S, so the helicity defect is linear in the numbers √det A\_k and sees the inertia operator through nothing else. An operator is Euler–Arnold only if an explicit matrix M(C) has an entrywise positive null vector: Euler–Arnold vertices give corank exactly one with that null vector, while cascade operators give full rank for two, three and four species on five mode sets, and for Tao's own coefficients the null vector exists but fails positivity. The species count never enters the criterion, which is why it reaches the case Paper III could not. The criterion is also unchanged by viscosity: the dissipative term is common to both classes and cancels from the comparison, so the exclusion holds uniformly in ν, and a cascade operator produces helicity at a rate that survives the inviscid limit. [Preprint](/preprints/momentum-helicity-separation.pdf)

**V. A Beale–Kato–Majda Criterion for Right-Invariant H^s Metrics on SDiff(T³)** — 13 pp. Not a rigidity theorem: Tao observed that no Beale–Kato–Majda criterion was available for generalised Euler equations and did not attempt one, and this paper proves it. Fix the bracket of SDiff(T³) and let the inertia operator run over the isotropic multipliers a(|k|) = |k|^{2s}, so that s = 0 is Euler. For s > 0 a solution persists as long as ∫₀ᵀ ‖w‖\_{L^p} dt < ∞ for a single exponent p > 3/(2s), where w = curl m is the momentum vorticity. Two ingredients: w obeys the *unmodified* vortex-stretching equation for every s, so all the s-dependence sits in recovering the velocity from w — a multiplier of order −1−2s — and for s > 0 the resulting generalised Biot–Savart law is smoothing, giving ‖∇u‖\_{L^∞} ≲ ‖w‖\_{L^p} with no logarithm. Classical Beale–Kato–Majda is the endpoint s → 0⁺, where p escapes to infinity and the Brezis–Gallouët–Wainger logarithm becomes unavoidable. The paper also records what the criterion does not do: the conserved energy dominates ‖∇u‖\_{L^∞} only for s ≥ 5/2, recovering the Bruveris–Vialard geodesic completeness threshold from the analytic side and confirming that the criterion alone moves nothing. [Preprint](/preprints/beale-kato-majda-criterion.pdf)

## Status and attribution

The central results are proved. Paper I's finite hypotheses are verified case by case by an accompanying script rather than established in general, and Papers II, III and IV mark their computational propositions as numerical — two in Paper II, nine in Paper III, nine in Paper IV. Paper V has none: everything in it is proved. The question and the direction are mine. The derivations, the numerical work, the reconstruction of Tao's cascade, and a first draft were produced with substantial AI assistance under my direction, and I am responsible for the contents, including any errors.
