*Eight preprints, September 2026. All eight were produced with substantial AI assistance under my direction — the full statement is at the [bottom of this page](#status-and-attribution).*

## The program

By Arnold's theorem the Euler equations are not just a bilinear operator obeying an energy identity: they are the Euler–Arnold equation of a Lie algebra with an inertia operator. Tao's averaged Navier–Stokes equation replaces the Euler nonlinearity with an averaged operator that blows up in finite time, and it satisfies the same energy identity. The program runs in three parts.

**Papers I–IV** ask whether that operator can be an Euler–Arnold operator — whether the blowup mechanism is compatible with the geometric structure the Euler equations actually have, or only with the energy identity they happen to satisfy. The answer is that it is not — for Tao's operator, and for translation-covariant symbols. It is not a statement that no Euler–Arnold operator blows up: that stronger reading is false, since a second theorem of Tao produces one whose inertia operator is inhomogeneous of the same order as Euler's. The first three papers press the energy identity as far as it goes and stop short of the general case; the fourth changes the invariant and settles it.

**Paper V and Papers VI–VIII** take up the two questions Paper I leaves for the isotropic family a(|k|) = |k|^{2s} on SDiff(T³), with s = 0 the Euler equation. Question 7.2 asks how far the geodesic-completeness threshold s ≥ 5/2 can be displaced; any attack on it needs a continuation criterion, none existed, and Paper V proves one. Question 7.1 asks whether some isotropic inertia operator makes the equation blow up from smooth data. Papers VI–VIII take it through three reductions, each chosen because the known and conjectured blowups of Euler live there: two-and-a-half-dimensional flows, axisymmetric flows with swirl, and a one-dimensional model at the wall of a cylinder.

**Where it ends.** Question 7.1 is not answered. Paper VI finds no collapse in the geometry most likely to produce one. Paper VII shows that the geometry of the proved Euler blowup is unavailable without a boundary. Paper VIII supplies the boundary and proves a sharp dichotomy — blowup below s = 1/2, regularity above — for a one-dimensional model on a cylinder. That result is about the model; the question on R³ and T³ is untouched by it.

## The eight papers

**I. Rigidity of the Euler–Arnold Form under Energy-Preserving Deformation** — 21 pp. Two classes of deformation of the Euler vertex are treated: triad reweightings, and vertices twisted by scalar Fourier multipliers on the three legs. On SDiff(T³) the energy-preserving triad reweightings that remain Euler–Arnold are exactly the global rescalings, and the family does not reopen for anisotropic, matrix-valued inertia operators; on Zeitlin's su(N) truncation of SDiff(T²) the corresponding family is two-dimensional instead, which is what gives the three-dimensional statement its content. Tao's operator lies in neither class and is reached separately: because his profiles all live in one thin annulus, a cascade triple is compatible with the Euler bracket only if it carries three distinct species, and twelve of the sixteen non-zero structure constants of his Table 1 carry fewer. Corollary 6.5 sharpens this — the twelve include every coupling that moves energy between scales, so what survives is single-scale and provably bounded. Section 7 states the scope: the known routes to blowup for such a geodesic flow either break the homogeneity of the metric, as Tao's second construction does, or drive the Jacobian of the Lagrangian flow map to zero, which volume preservation forbids — so the translation-covariant case in which these symbol arguments are stated is the one that remains open. It poses Questions 7.1 and 7.2, records that the completeness range reaches its endpoint and, through Osgood's lemma, a logarithm below it, and explicitly closes off one route to Question 7.2 — through the momentum helicity — that suggests itself and does not work. [Preprint](/preprints/euler-arnold-rigidity.pdf)

**II. The Isotropic Directional Obstruction for Cascade Operators** — 14 pp. The four couplings that pass Paper I's frequency-support test are settled in the isotropic category, by an argument using the directional structure of the Euler vertex and nothing about frequency geometry: on any non-degenerate triad, no rank-one tensor is an isotropic Euler–Arnold vertex. Since a basic cascade operator contributes exactly one rank-one tensor per block, no basic cascade operator is an isotropic Euler–Arnold operator — irrespective of species count, of the thin annulus, and of whether its frequencies close up. For general anisotropic inertia operators the complementary statement is proved: no block of an Euler–Arnold vertex vanishes faster than κ⁻¹ in the joint spread κ of the three inertia operators, and that rate is sharp, while Tao's surviving coupling needs a block to vanish identically. The paper also fixes the shape any treatment of finite sums must take — the only linear functionals vanishing on the Euler–Arnold class are the componentwise energy identity, which every cascade operator satisfies by construction, so the sum case cannot be decided linearly, nor on one triad at all. [Preprint](/preprints/isotropic-directional-obstruction.pdf)

**III. Cross-Triad Rigidity for Cascade Operators** — 8 pp. What Paper II leaves is whether one fixed coefficient set can reproduce an Euler–Arnold vertex on *all* triads at once. For a two-species cascade this is a gauge-trivialisation problem, answered negatively for every scalar inertia multiplier. A first invariant — the ratios of the blocks' Cayley hyperdeterminants — excludes all exponents but s = 0 and s = −2, Euler and its vorticity dual; that degeneracy is shown to be structural rather than accidental, since on the relevant stratum the hyperdeterminant reduces to the product of the block's four entries. It is also shown to be only a weakness of that invariant: the correct condition, that all the vertices lie in a single GL(2)³ orbit, fails at s = 0 and s = −2 as well. The proof turns on an invariant *condition* rather than an invariant function — the rank of a flattening — which is blind to the degeneracy that silences the hyperdeterminant, and settles all scalar multipliers at once. [Preprint](/preprints/cross-triad-rigidity.pdf)

**IV. Momentum Helicity Separates Cascade Operators from Euler–Arnold Operators** — 13 pp. Papers I–III are all built on the energy identity, which is exactly the invariant Tao's cancellation condition is designed to preserve, so no linear functional can separate the two classes — that is Paper II's Corollary 5.4, and it is why Paper III ends at a dimension count rather than a decision. This paper changes the invariant. Three-dimensional ideal flow conserves a second quantity that Tao's condition does not impose: the helicity of the momentum m = Au, a Casimir of the Lie–Poisson structure and therefore conserved by every Euler–Arnold flow, whatever the inertia operator. The resulting condition collapses to linear algebra — on k⊥ the curl acts as i|k|J for a quarter turn J, and SJS = det(S)J for symmetric S, so the helicity defect is linear in the numbers √det A\_k and sees the inertia operator through nothing else. An operator is Euler–Arnold only if an explicit matrix M(C) has an entrywise positive null vector: Euler–Arnold vertices give corank exactly one with that null vector, while cascade operators give full rank for two, three and four species on five mode sets, and for Tao's own coefficients the null vector exists but fails positivity. The species count never enters the criterion, which is why it reaches the case Paper III could not. The criterion is also unchanged by viscosity: the dissipative term is common to both classes and cancels from the comparison, so the exclusion holds uniformly in ν. The paper also bounds its own method. Applied before the time derivative, the same reduction gives the *value* of the helicity as a linear functional of those determinants, so the entire Casimir content of an inertia operator is that one vector, and the criterion is shown to be sharp for it — it extracts everything this Casimir can give. Along the fibres of that map, two free parameters per leg pair, the criterion and the helicity stay constant to machine precision while the vortex-stretching production does not. [Preprint](/preprints/momentum-helicity-separation.pdf)

**V. A Beale–Kato–Majda Criterion for Right-Invariant H^s Metrics on SDiff(T³)** — 20 pp. Not a rigidity theorem: Tao observed that no Beale–Kato–Majda criterion was available for generalised Euler equations and did not attempt one, and this paper proves it. For s > 0 a solution persists as long as ∫₀ᵀ ‖w‖\_{L^p} dt < ∞ for a single exponent p > 3/(2s), where w = curl m is the momentum vorticity, and that range is sharp — the critical exponent is genuinely excluded, not lost to the method. Two ingredients: w obeys the *unmodified* vortex-stretching equation for every s, so all the s-dependence sits in recovering the velocity from w — a multiplier of order −1−2s — and for s > 0 the resulting generalised Biot–Savart law is smoothing, giving ‖∇u‖\_{L^∞} ≲ ‖w‖\_{L^p} with no logarithm. Classical Beale–Kato–Majda is the endpoint s → 0⁺, where p escapes to infinity and the Brezis–Gallouët–Wainger logarithm becomes unavoidable. The family itself is not new: its inviscid case is the Navier–Stokes-α-like system of Olson and Titi, whose analytic proof of regularity for s ≥ 5/2 predates the Bruveris–Vialard geodesic-completeness threshold at the same place. The paper records what its criterion does not do — the conserved energy dominates ‖∇u‖\_{L^∞} only from s ≥ 5/2, so the criterion alone moves nothing — and shows that everything but one lemma carries over to anisotropic and matrix-valued symbols homogeneous of degree 2s. [Preprint](/preprints/beale-kato-majda-criterion.pdf)

**VI. Two-and-a-Half-Dimensional Flows of the H^s Euler–Arnold Equations: A Self-Gravitating Boussinesq System** — 20 pp. Data independent of one coordinate stay so, but the classical two-and-a-half-dimensional cancellation of Euler survives only at s = 0. The reduced dynamics is a Lie–Poisson system on the same semidirect product as the two-dimensional Boussinesq and ideal MHD equations: the swirl momentum θ is transported, and the vertical vorticity of the momentum is forced by J(Λ^{−2s}θ, θ) — Boussinesq convection in which the density generates its own gravitational potential. Its conserved sup norm is scaling-critical exactly at s = 1/2, where for Boussinesq it is supercritical. Proved: global regularity for s ≥ 2, and a logarithm below it; for s > 1/2 a continuation criterion in ∇θ alone; for 1 ≤ s < 2, that blowup can occur only through the high–high interaction in a single Jacobian, which is the precise obstruction in a range subcritical for a Casimir and supercritical for the energy; and that no steady state is energy–Casimir stable, with layered states Jeans-unstable. The obstruction is one of unmixing — the source of the vorticity is bounded by a Casimir for all time, linear stirring cannot amplify it however strong, and what is needed is folding. A spectral study in the Hou–Luo stagnation-point geometry at s = 1/4, validated to round-off at up to 2048² points, finds no collapse: a generic corner filaments with anti-feedback, and the Hou–Luo corner, after a phase of positive feedback that projected blowup, rebounds. That weakens, but does not close, a route to a positive answer to Question 7.1. [Preprint](/preprints/two-and-a-half-dimensional-flows.pdf)

**VII. Axisymmetric Flows with Swirl of the H^s Euler–Arnold Equations: A Reduction, Two Thresholds, and a Missing Wall** — 7 pp. The known and conjectured blowups of Euler are axisymmetric with swirl, so the family is reduced to that class on R³. The swirl circulation of the momentum is transported, and the azimuthal vorticity of the momentum is forced by one Jacobian with the angular velocity — the azimuthal component of Cauchy's formula — which, unlike the two-and-a-half-dimensional class, keeps a Boussinesq-type linear part for every s > 0. The conserved sup norm of the swirl circulation gives two scaling thresholds: critical at s = 1 for concentration on the axis and at s = 1/2 away from it, dropping to s = 1/4 for swirl odd in z, the parity of the known blowup candidates. At s = 0 the on-axis count reproduces the scaling reported for Hou's interior candidate. The missing wall: reflection in z supplies a free-slip plane, but no symmetry of R³ supplies the wall on which the Chen–Hou blowup lives. A spectrally exact solver, validated against Hou's interior scenario to about 60% of Hou's blowup time, shows the early growth weakening monotonically in s and reversing between s = 1/4 and s = 1/2. [Preprint](/preprints/axisymmetric-flows-with-swirl.pdf)

**VIII. A One-Dimensional Wall Model for the H^s Euler–Arnold Equations: Blowup Below s = 1/2, Regularity Above** — 16 pp. Paper VII shows that no symmetry of R³ supplies a wall, so this paper puts the family on a cylinder. The wall needs boundary conditions for the inertia: with Dirichlet conditions on the swirl the Luo–Hou scenario cannot occur; with slip conditions it can, and restricting to the wall as Hou and Luo do gives a one-dimensional model — theirs, with the velocity law smoothed by 2s derivatives. For this model and its local version after Choi, Kiselev and Yao, the paper proves a sharp dichotomy in α = 2s: for 0 < α < 1 there is smooth sign-definite data that blows up in finite time, and for α ≥ 1 every solution with data of that class is global. The threshold s = 1/2 is exactly Paper VII's off-axis scaling threshold. Blowup survives a depletion term, absent at α = 0, which neither the Choi–Kiselev–Yao argument nor the functional argument of Choi, Hou, Kiselev, Luo, Šverák and Yao controls; it is controlled here by a Riccati comparison along characteristics. The paper records that its own registered expectation — that the full velocity law could not be pushed to the threshold — was wrong. With the buoyancy the reduction actually produces, the parity of the swirl decides: s = 1/2 for even swirl, s = 1/4 for odd, which is Luo and Hou's parity. In the odd class blowup below s = 1/4 is proved for both laws and regularity above it for the local law; for the full law with odd swirl, 1/4 ≤ s < 1/2 remains open. These are theorems about a one-dimensional model on a bounded domain; the question on R³ and T³, where there is no wall, is not affected by them. [Preprint](/preprints/one-dimensional-wall-model.pdf)

## Status and attribution

**Proof status.** Paper I's finite hypotheses are verified case by case by an accompanying script rather than established in general. Papers II, III and IV mark their computational propositions as numerical — two, nine and eleven respectively. Paper V's theorems are proved, granted the commutator, product and kernel estimates it quotes, with numerical checks alongside them. Papers VI, VII and VIII prove their theorems and report numerical studies as evidence next to them, not in place of them; Paper VIII's checks were registered before they were run, and the runs added afterwards are labelled post hoc.

**Attribution.** Where a paper carries its own acknowledgement, that is the precise statement of who did what. In outline: the question and the direction of the program are mine, including the choice of target at each step. One thing that is not: for Paper V, the identification that a Beale–Kato–Majda criterion was the missing prerequisite — that none existed for this family, and that supplying one was the available next step — was made by Claude, the AI system I worked with, in the course of the work. The derivations, the proofs, the numerical work, the reconstruction of Tao's cascade, and a first draft of each paper were produced with substantial AI assistance under my direction, and I am responsible for the contents, including any errors. Not yet independently reviewed; corrections welcome.
